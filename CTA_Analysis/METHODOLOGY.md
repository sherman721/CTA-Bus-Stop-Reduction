# Methodology and assumptions — Phase 1

Select Tuesday September 22, 2026 via calendar and exceptions. Preserve distinct physical trips even if departure times coincide; use distinct departure times for descriptive headways. GTFS shape distances are treated as feet, consistent with coordinate scale; validate against shape geometry before final modeling. Periods are assigned by trip start; 06–09, 09–15, 15–19, otherwise other. No peak demand multipliers. Exact observation dates unknown.

## Field QC
Keep raw values and notes. Exclude boarding/alighting observation boundaries, out-of-coverage rows, eliminated stops, truck-delay notes and transition-related driver announcements from normal timing means. Original driver break belongs to 8:18 SB clearance log; copied note on 10:47 log does not invalidate numeric clearance. Blank with recorded pass time and no note is a presumed skip; blank without evidence is unknown. Numeric measurement without arrival remains eligible unless another exclusion applies. Coverage inferred from recorded evidence; review boundaries before corridor analysis. Friday and Monday samples are not paired. PM uses AM proxy in later model only.

## Travel-time model design (implemented in Phase 3)
1. GTFS anchored: scheduled baseline plus modeled change in stop-related delay.
2. Standalone TCQSM: free-flow running time plus stop delay and roadway running-time loss.
3. Field-informed: observed dwell and clearance, engineering deceleration and remaining acceleration; no causal before-after claim.

Common Poisson stopping probability p = 1-exp(-(B+A)/N), with daily activity distributed proportionally to scheduled trip exposure. Terminal stops mandatory. Independent Poisson board/alight assumption is an approximation; use expected maximum of simultaneous passenger service times, not maximum of means. Fixed door cycle 4 seconds per actual stop; boarding/alighting coefficients as specified in Phase 3. Acceleration 3.4 ft/s², deceleration 4.0 ft/s², assumed running speed 25 mph. Use actual stopping-event spacing to cap attainable speed; subtract uninterrupted travel time to compute incremental delay. Field clearance covers approximately 40 ft; replace first acceleration portion with measured clearance rather than double count it. Do not add door cycle to measured dwell.

TCRP RRD117, Estimating Bus Speed: Update of the TCQSM Method, DOI 10.17226/29469, equations 1–3 and Table 7: roadway coefficients in config are published recommended peak running-time losses (min/mile). Urban one lane mixed traffic 3, urban multilane 2, CBD mixed 3, suburban mixed 1. Off-peak application is an assumption. Running speed is assumed, not measured 95th percentile. 

Redistribute boarding and alighting separately with inverse-distance weights to nearest eligible retained stops in each direction, conserving total activity. The 1500 ft threshold is a behavioral allocation preference, not a physical barrier; see Phase 2. Walking at 1.33 m/s; along-route distance proxy, not pedestrian network. Compute passenger-load-weighted in-vehicle time and unweighted walking/waiting/ride totals; no OD-level benefit claims. Expected wait based on scheduled headways under random arrival assumption. Fixed service span and 9 minutes assumed layover per terminal. Distinguish theoretical frequency improvements from feasible block schedules and cash savings. Sensitivities disabled by default; mobility-specific walking, alternative stop probabilities, peak demand and generalized time are future work.

## Phase 2: scope and reconciliation
Only the dominant full-length directional patterns are modeled (83 trips each; seven partial trips excluded). All historical weekday demand is assigned to those trips, which may slightly overstate their loads. Historical IDs 8970 and 8615 map to 18836 and 18834 respectively. Roosevelt NB 8901 excluded from every analytical inventory; retain 8902 and raw schedule running times. Both CTA relocations ignored, including their walking and signal effects. CTA removal IDs are based on field notes.

NB Catalpa 8623 is temporarily absent from September GTFS. The permanent inventory restores it between Balmoral and Bryn Mawr using latitude interpolation along that segment. The scheduled inventory excludes it in all scenarios; its historical demand is redistributed to retained stops in each scenario, including the original. Compare walking and stopping changes against the corresponding original scope, not against zero. The standalone model will use the permanent scope; anchored models use scheduled scope. No savings are attributed to an additional physical Catalpa removal in the scheduled scope.

Redistribute boarding and alighting activity separately to the two final directional neighbors with inverse-distance weights. Exclude a neighbor beyond 1500 ft when the other is eligible; if neither qualifies, use the nearest and flag. Distances are along route, not pedestrian network. All historical demand is conserved. Poisson probability is 1-exp(-(B+A)/83). Expected stops are sums of these probabilities, not counts of designated stops. Terminal stopping is mandatory for the future motion model, irrespective of passenger activity. Walking hours in scenario_summary are absolute additional walking relative to the historical stop locations, not yet net scenario differences.

## Phase 3: travel-time engine
Implemented in travel_time.py. Boarding 2.75 s/person (smart card), rear-door alighting 1.75 s/person (TCQSM 2013 Exhibit 6-4, reproduced at https://dspacemainprd01.lib.uwaterloo.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/cc70056b-afa0-49a8-8c50-b0ef604651de/content ; smart-card default reaffirmed in https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/29448/chapter/5). Expected simultaneous service E[max(2.75 B,1.75 A)] computed by independent Poisson sums with negligible tail truncation. No mobility-specific or cash fare adjustments. Terminals mandatory in motion, terminal dwell excluded from departure-to-arrival runtime. For each possible consecutive stopping pair i,j, probability p_i p_j product(1-p_k) for intermediate stops. Peak attainable velocity min(v,sqrt(2 L/(1/a+1/d))); travel time u/a+u/d+remaining cruise distance/v. Sum expected pair travel times; subtract freeflow. GTFS anchor retains original scheduled time less modeled incremental cost reduction. Standalone adds freeflow, stopping costs and 3 min/mile roadway loss, applied all day as a simplifying assumption. Field model anchors to GTFS and replaces theoretical first 40 ft acceleration time with observed clearance mean where attainable acceleration distance reaches 40 ft. Remaining motion unchanged; short distances not eligible for replacement. Field dwell scales theoretical expected dwell by observed mean times CTA expected interior stops divided by CTA theoretical dwell, separately by direction and period. This preserves passenger concentration effects, but assumes measured dwell includes door operations and is representative of the modeled route. AM proxy for PM, midday proxy for other periods. No separate field roadway increment: avoid double counting GTFS background delay. Clearance may include traffic/signal delay not truly avoidable; field estimates are not causal and may overstate savings. Standalone uses permanent inventory including Catalpa; others scheduled inventory. Outputs are preliminary trip-level and direction-level runtime results; passenger and service analysis still pending.

## Phase 4: passenger travel time
Reconstruct aggregate daily directional loads from scenario boardings minus alightings, scaling alightings by the boarding/alighting total ratio solely for load balance (rounding-level discrepancy). Assert nonnegative loads. Use the original inventory's distances as a common segment grid. Integrate the acceleration/cruise/braking time profile over each segment for every possible consecutive stopping pair, weighted by its probability. Allocate background running time uniformly by distance; anchored and field residuals are trip-specific and preserved across scenarios. Field clearance excess is assigned to the first segment after the stopping location. Multiply segment expected time by mean passenger load, and dwell by through passengers plus half of boarding/alighting activity (approximation). Terminal dwell excluded. This aggregate mean-load approach does not model correlation between onboard load and stopping events or individual OD journeys. Daily demand distributed evenly across full-length trips. Compare scenario-specific passenger loads and times, so changed boarding/alighting locations affect riding distance as well as walking. Assert reconstructed vehicle runtimes match Phase 3. Waiting-time change is assumed zero in this fixed-frequency stage, not an exact stop-specific schedule prediction. Frequency improvement and irregular headways are subsequent analysis. Positive net savings = ride hours saved minus added walking hours. Fuel/energy savings will be estimated separately from operating time, with propulsion-specific acceleration and idling assumptions; avoid double counting fuel embedded in operating rates.

## Phase 5: continuous service reinvestment
Fixed daily cycle-minute budget: 83 round trips times mean directional runtime sum plus 18 minutes layover. Solve f*C(f)=C(1,original) by bisection, where f scales trip counts and Poisson means are divided by f. Field calibration remains fixed to observed CTA configuration at original frequency. Random-arrival terminal waiting proxy uses sum(h²)/(2 sum(h)) within separate early, AM, midday, PM and late windows; boundary intervals omitted. Scale intervals by 1/f, assume uniform proportional frequency increase and unchanged demand. This is a continuous resource equivalent, not a feasible block schedule or exact stop-level waiting calculation. Preliminary net passenger time holds Phase 4 ride impacts fixed; frequency-induced ride changes still require segment-level recomputation. No labor savings credited to reinvested time. Fuel analysis pending; distinguish avoided acceleration, idling and additional mileage.

## Phase 6: stopping-frequency sensitivity and break-even
Apply p*=p+alpha(1-p) to every interior stop in both original and consolidated scenarios after redistribution and after dividing demand by the candidate trip count. Terminals remain mandatory. Alpha is a researcher-defined extra-stop probability conditional on otherwise skipping, not a fitted Poisson parameter. Passenger service remains unconditional expected maximum of simultaneous boarding and alighting times; extra stops incur door and motion delay even without passenger activity. Field calibration is held fixed at the regular-Poisson CTA reference to avoid changing the empirical delay coefficient during sensitivity testing. This is a hypothetical stress test, not a prediction of actual non-passenger stops. GTFS anchoring retains the scheduled original runtime at each alpha; the residual background time therefore changes across alpha, but is shared within each comparison. Standalone uses fixed roadway loss. Segment loads are daily aggregate loads, unchanged by frequency under fixed demand; per-bus demand and dwell are recomputed. Frequency solves f*C(f)=C_original with equal directional multipliers and fixed terminal layovers; waiting scales by 1/f under the existing terminal-headway proxy. Recalculate in-vehicle time at f, replacing Phase 5's fixed-ride approximation. Evaluate alpha at increments of 0.1 and solve first bracketed zero with Brent's method; report zero if already beneficial, and retain all-stop endpoint. No claim of global monotonicity outside sampled intervals. Outputs: break_even.csv, stopping_sensitivity.csv. Positive net hours indicate savings. All comparisons preserve Catalpa scope and ignore relocations as before.

Field plausibility: field_stopping_check.py matches observed stopped/skipped interior records to post-consolidation scheduled CTA stop IDs and directions. Exclude boundary, unknown, not observed and removed records; retain known stops with unusual delays for binary stopping status only. Compare mean predicted probability on the same records to observed fraction. Repeated observations are clustered within 12 trips, and historical demand is all-day; this is descriptive, not an independent validation or inferential test. Implied alpha=(observed-predicted)/(1-predicted) may be negative as a descriptive diagnostic. Do not compare raw field served fraction directly to original-route threshold.

## Phase 7: operating resources and energy break-even
Use Phase 6 alpha=0 results. Daily full-length mileage is the sum of GTFS trip distances divided by 5280; excludes partial trips, deadhead and garage movements. Cycle includes two nine-minute terminal layovers (researcher assumption; CTA Charging Forward 2022 describes typical 3–15 minute layovers). Fixed-frequency released running hours = 83 × round-trip minutes saved / 60; these are not paid hours saved. With reinvestment, net cycle resource change = 83 × (baseline cycle − frequency multiplier × new cycle)/60, approximately zero by construction. Additional revenue miles = baseline miles × (multiplier − 1). A sweep of fixed scheduled departure intervals extended by modeled runtime and nine-minute recovery gives a concurrent vehicle occupancy lower bound, not a feasible block schedule; ignores deadhead, interlining, terminal matching and labor rules. For reinvestment rows the occupancy columns still refer to fixed departures, not the hypothetical increased-frequency schedule.

Fuel/energy: if baseline daily energy is E and per-mile intensity falls by fraction q, new energy = E × frequency multiplier × (1−q). Break-even intensity reduction = 1−1/frequency multiplier. This is a threshold, not an estimate of achieved fuel economy. No route-specific fuel consumption, vehicle mix, marginal idle consumption, regenerative braking or fuel price verified; no dollar savings claimed. For unchanged service, any positive intensity reduction saves energy. Labor savings require actual shift/block restructuring; multiplying released hours by an assumed marginal hourly cost is only a conditional valuation. Avoid double counting fuel in all-inclusive operating rates. Stop maintenance savings and removal/reinvestment costs require asset inventory and unit costs; discuss qualitatively pending evidence.

## Phase 8: optional user-supplied financial and consumption analysis
No financial or fuel/energy consumption estimates are required for the class project. Physical resource and energy-intensity break-even outputs remain in the core pipeline. The optional calculator requires explicit scenario, annual equivalent weekdays, currency and provenance. Labor valuation uses user-confirmed paid hours actually recoverable, bounded by modeled net cycle resource hours; reinvested hours cannot be credited again. Mileage maintenance uses marginal nonenergy, nonlabor cost per mile times baseline minus scenario mileage; stop-related vehicle wear is not independently estimated. Energy uses user-supplied baseline and scenario consumption intensities in the same unit, multiplied by respective daily revenue miles; the difference is annualized and valued at a supplied common unit price. Intensities must account for acceleration, idling and propulsion differences if relevant; no inferred savings from time alone. Stop maintenance uses a supplied net annual saving from an asset inventory, and one-time removal and amenity costs are reported separately. No combined total is produced because overlapping cost categories and capital versus recurring costs require independent review. Null modules mean not evaluated, not zero. No default unit costs or annualization factors. All inputs are conditional, not verified CTA financial data.

## Distributional walking impacts
`distributional_analysis.py` uses the existing direction-specific redistribution allocations, defaulting to permanent inventory. Each removed stop's mean added distance is sum(activity times distance)/sum(activity); daily passenger-hours are sum(activity times distance in meters)/walking speed/3600. Distance categories use allocation-level activity, not stop-level means. The scheduled option includes baseline temporary-closure redistribution and is therefore an absolute burden rather than incremental consolidation-only burden. Map defaults to permanent scope. These are boarding/alighting events, not unique riders, and along-route distances are proxies rather than pedestrian-network paths. No demographic equity inference or segment riding-benefit map is produced. Source: existing reconciled inventory, redistribution and scenario summary outputs; no new external assumptions.
